# Commercial licence

AI-Gateway is published under the AGPL-3.0-or-later. That licence is free and
permanent, and for most users it is enough. This page is for the cases where it
is not.

## Who needs a commercial licence

**You offer the gateway to other people over a network.** A hosted gateway, a
SaaS product with this inside, a managed service for clients — the AGPL requires
you to publish the complete source of that service under the same terms. A
commercial licence lifts that requirement.

**You embed it in a product you ship to customers** and cannot release your own
source.

**Your organisation forbids AGPL by policy.** Many banks, telecoms and public
bodies do, regardless of how the software is used. A commercial licence is
usually the shortest way through that review.

**You do not need one** if the gateway runs inside your own company for your own
employees, however many of them there are, and however much you modify it. That
case is fully covered by the AGPL, and always will be.

## What the commercial licence grants

- The right to use, modify and distribute the gateway without the AGPL's
  source-disclosure obligation.
- The right to offer it as a network service under your own brand.
- Priority handling of bug reports, and a say in what gets built next.

## What it does not grant

- Ownership of the project or any exclusivity.
- A warranty beyond what is written in the signed agreement.
- Support hours, unless a separate support agreement says so.

## Pricing

Set per case, based on how the software is used — internal product, service sold
to third parties, number of deployments — not on seat count. Perpetual licence
for a fixed version, or a subscription that includes updates.

For a service in Kazakhstan or Central Asia the licence is usually combined with
help getting the deployment through local data-protection requirements, since
that is the reason the gateway exists in the first place.

## How to ask

Open an issue titled **"Commercial licence"** in this repository, or write to the
address in the repository owner's GitHub profile. Useful things to include:

- what the gateway would be part of;
- whether third parties would reach it over a network;
- expected scale — requests per day, number of deployments;
- whether the data is personal data, and in which jurisdiction.

A short answer with a price range usually comes back the same week.

## Why dual licensing at all

The AGPL keeps the project honest: anyone can read the code that sees every
prompt, verify what it does with personal data, and run it without asking
permission. That is not a marketing position — for a tool of this kind it is a
precondition for being trusted at all.

The commercial licence pays for the work that keeps it maintained. Companies
that build a business on top of it contribute; everyone else uses it for free.
